# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Phase2 employees — primarily whoever organizes lunch for the office on a given day. They open the admin panel a couple of times a day: once to launch a session (pick a restaurant, assemble the crew), and again to confirm orders went through. The audience is small (a single office), familiar with the tool, and in a flow state — they want to get lunch sorted and get back to work.

## Product Purpose

Lunchinator takes the "where should we eat?" debate off the table. The organizer picks a restaurant or spins for a random one, selects who's joining, and the bot handles the rest — opening a Slack group DM, posting the DoorDash link, tracking RSVPs, and running the countdown. The admin panel manages the restaurant pool, default deadline, and crew configuration.

## Brand Personality

Playful, techy, self-aware. This is a tool that knows it's a lunch bot and leans into it — not a serious enterprise product pretending to be important. It should feel like something a smart engineering team built for themselves: efficient and opinionated, with just enough personality to make it feel like a product rather than a script.

## Anti-references

- Legacy enterprise tools (gray, cluttered, Bootstrap-era)
- Generic SaaS dashboards (white bg, blue sidebar, data tables, Notion/Linear clone)
- Over-designed marketing pages (gradient blobs, glassmorphism, hero metrics)
- Overly consumer/food-delivery aesthetic (bubbly, emoji-heavy, pastel)

## Design Principles

1. **The tool disappears into the task.** The UI is in service of getting to lunch — every element should earn its presence. Remove what doesn't help the user act.
2. **Personality through precision.** Playfulness comes from confident, opinionated choices (tone, timing, specific UI moments) — not decoration, animation for its own sake, or over-reliance on emoji.
3. **Restrained by default, expressive in moments.** Phase2's dark palette and cyan accent are already the brand; don't decorate the whole surface — save the color for actions and state.
4. **Consistency earns trust.** Same button shape, same form vocabulary, same icon style everywhere. Variety is noise in a tool.
5. **The admin feels like an extension of the Slack experience.** It's the backstage of the Slack card — the same voice, same brand, different modality.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Best effort — no formal WCAG requirement. Minimum: body text readable (4.5:1 contrast), keyboard navigable, no seizure-triggering animations.
